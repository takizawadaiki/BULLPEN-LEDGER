Pitcher-season fixed effects, entry conditions and recent performance. Effects are percentage points in K−uBB rate, holding included variables fixed.

95% pitcher-clustered intervals. Correlated workload variables can be difficult to separate. An interval crossing zero is compatible with effects in either direction; it does not prove no fatigue exists.

Selection still matters.

We observe pitchers who were used. A manager may already have rested those most fatigued. Unavailable pitchers, warm-ups and private recovery measures are missing.

A decision-support boundary

This project measures recorded workload and subsequent performance. It cannot identify a pitcher’s underlying fatigue, diagnose injury risk, or establish what would happen if a manager changed the rest schedule.

The practical use is an auditable conversation: who pitched recently, what was recorded, what changed, and what additional information would a coach need?

Cohort, rest days and missing data

Relief appearances are pitching sequence greater than one, with positive batters faced and at least five earlier relief appearances in that season. A retrospective season-role filter requires pitching appearances to be at least half of all games played. This excludes position-player pitching; it also limits evaluation to this defined cohort.

Workload sums recorded pitches over the previous calendar dates, including starts and actual continuation dates of suspended games. Suspended-game outcomes and pitcher-days with two appearances are excluded from modeling. Minor-league and rehab appearances, warm-ups and bullpen sessions are not available; gaps in MLB records must not be interpreted as confirmed rest. Missing pitch counts on non-plate-appearance events count as zero; every completed plate appearance has a recorded count. Missing temperature uses the development-sample median.

Model and inference details

Outcome = (strikeouts − walks + intentional walks) / all batters faced. The prior baseline pools earlier non-suspended relief outcomes with 100 pseudo-batters at a fixed 15% rate; it resets each season. Both ridge models use development-only standardization, penalty 100 and batters-faced weights. No tuning on the 2025 test set.

Association estimates use weighted demeaning within pitcher-season, WLS and pitcher-clustered confidence intervals with absorbed-effect degrees-of-freedom correction and a cluster-t critical value on development seasons. They are distinct from the predictive ridge coefficients. Descriptive and velocity intervals resample pitchers. Fixed random seed: 71. Entry context is measured when a pitcher enters; these are not day-before forecasts.

What an R&D follow-up should test

Integrate warm-up counts, exact recovery hours, travel, role and roster availability. Add opponent and handedness adjustment, pitch shape and movement, and pitch-type-specific league-wide velocity. Use rolling-origin validation and a genuinely new season before operational use.

Study the decision to deploy as well as the performance of deployed pitchers. A causal rest-policy claim needs a design that addresses that selection process; a workload correlation alone is insufficient.
